              Match

              A key per kick; how long you hold it sets the power – your player winds up while you hold, so the ball is struck the moment you let go. On a ball coming to you, press twice to fill the gauge twice as fast, three times for full power (a big first-time clearance). Click a key to rebind it. (Pad and touch: a target button – team-mate, run path, goal, self – and a height.)

              Targets: Team-mate plays it to where he is now, Run path into the space ahead of him, Goal shoots, Self plays it to yourself (on the keyboard: Q / A / S through, lob and ground passes, D shoot, W knock it on, X + W lift it, E control it – or, the ball at your feet, a trick). The stick picks which team-mate (or where the shot goes). Heights: ground = along the grass to his feet (a low shot), mid = chest-high (a driven ball), high = head-high for a header (a shot under the bar; little power + high = a chip).

              Shooting: it goes where the stick points – the spot on the goal line along it, up to a metre wide of a post (bend it back in with aftertouch). Power is pace, not height – a full-power shot is hard to keep down and less accurate, and the further out you shoot from, the wider it can go; every shot swerves a little – bend it back with aftertouch. A high shot goes in just under the bar. R2 + Goal = finesse (curled). When the ball is in the air, a Goal key volleys, heads or – facing away – bicycle-kicks it.

              Back to goal: a Goal key turns with the ball and shoots – full power, but the turn takes a moment (quicker for a small, nimble player) and you can be tackled. Close in, a quick tap back-heels it instead – at once, but weak. A ball in the air: an overhead kick (flick it up yourself with Self-high first).

              Self: ground = knock it on and chase it – a tap just nudges it (sideways / back on the stick: step-over feint, drag-back), mid = pop it up to chest height, high = flick it up / scoop it over a challenge – and pressed again as it comes down, keepy-ups (foot, knee / thigh, chest; held long, the head). When the ball comes to you, it controls it towards the stick with foot, chest or head. Control (E): a ball coming taken dead at your feet – chest, thigh, foot, or a jump to head it down – as the keeper in his box, hold it (or R2) to catch: without it a ball at you only hits you. Tricks (E with the ball at your feet or juggling, pad R3): at your feet an elastico (stick to a side), la croqueta (diagonally ahead), a back-heel flick (back) or a rainbow flick (forward); your own keepy-up dropping with the stick pushed, a sombrero over the man in front; juggling, around the world (press as it drops past your knee) or a neck stall (press a metre above your neck; W or E pops it off) – timed: a bubble says perfect, good or mistimed. R2 with a pass key: a no-look pass. X / Z held with a kick key: a level higher / lower (Z + D a low shot, Shift + D or X + D the top corner, X + A to his head).

              Aftertouch (Sensible Soccer style): right after a kick, push the stick left/right of the ball's flight to bend it that way, back to lift it, forward to dip it. Keep holding your aim and nothing happens – turn the stick, or let go and press again.

              Shooting practice: a new room has an AI keeper in Team B's goal; in the warm-up with only AI keepers on the pitch besides you, the keepers serve every ball back to you – no kick-offs, set pieces or goal celebrations. Any other warm-up is played by the rules like a match, without a clock or a score. Click outside the room panel (or press Esc) to tuck it away.

              Defence: hold a Team-mate key to press the ball carrier (auto standing tackle), hold a Self key to jockey (stay goal-side at arm's length, facing him, no lunge), hold R2 to guard (keep facing the ball, side-stepping into position), Goal = slide tackle (from behind may be a foul; keeper: dive), Run path = send your keeper out. When a shot or pass comes at you, press any kick key to block it – your player throws out a leg, his body or jumps to head it (the keeper uses his hands). The touch buttons and an on-screen hint switch to these whenever you're defending.

              Keeper with the ball: the ground pass rolls it out, the lob pass throws it (a drop kick when it's far), shoot punts it.

              Passing past opponents: a ground pass is angled round an opponent near its line when your team-mate can still get to it. Otherwise it goes as close past him as it can: played straight at him it's usually stopped (now and then it goes through his legs), just past his foot it gets by more often.

              Switch player: with the other side on the ball, control moves to your nearest AI team-mate by itself, and C (Select / View) moves it by hand – tap again for the next one. The host can turn it off.

              Team-mates: a Team-mate or Run key while an AI team-mate has the ball calls for it – your keeper too.

              Receive assist: when the ball is played to you your player is guided to meet it; a ring on the grass marks the spot (yellow = header height). Hold a kick key as it arrives for a first-time pass, volley or header.

              High balls – how you meet it decides what happens. The assist keeps you on the ball's line; you choose with the stick. Run at the ball (stick back along its flight): with a kick key, a header as high as you can jump; with no key, you cushion it on the head or chest. Run with it (stick along its flight): with a kick key, a volley if you get there in time, otherwise a flying header (facing away from goal, a bicycle kick); with no key, you take it on the thigh or foot, in your stride. Neither: you take it at whatever height it arrives. Your choice holds until the ball is touched again.

              Touch: drag anywhere on the left half for the stick – how far sets the pace. On the right are three round buttons: a big Shoot, with Pass and Through beside it (the keyboard's D, S, Q): slide the finger up on one for a level higher (further, two – a lob, the top corner), down for a level lower; how long you hold sets the power. Switching player and the keeper's catch happen by themselves. The rest of the right half is the skill stick: a tap lifts the ball (again as it drops: keepy-ups), a flick is a trick – the way you flick picks it, as the stick does.

              Quick chat: keys 1–5 (or 💬 on touch) – Pass! · Shoot! · Nice one! · Sorry! · Good game!

              The study: an AI that plays for excitement

              The goal. AI players that make matches exciting – not just ones that win. Later every AI player gets a small neural network, trained by reinforcement learning with excitement as its reward (scoring still counts, conceding too – so neither side gives goals away for a show).

              Where we are – step 1 of 3: measuring excitement. Every AI player is still the classic, hand-written AI – there are no neural players yet. What's new is the measure:

              • Threat – each side's chance of scoring in the next 10 s, from a model learned on 1,500 AI matches.
              • Expected result – A's chance of winning (a draw counts half), from the score, the time left and both threats.
              • Excitement (0–100) – how fast the expected result swings: sudden swings count most.
              • Events – each pass, shot, tackle, save, dive… valued by what it did to his side's threat and result.

              The question: does the measure agree with you? Your ratings are the ground truth it gets fitted to.

              What to do

              1. Spectate an AI match: room panel → Spectate, add AI to both teams, Start match. Open the game as ?p2p=1 so your tab hosts it and M stops it. While you spectate, the whole match is kept.
              2. M – look back. The chart shows the meter (filled), the threats (A blue, B orange) and what happened above. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan.
              3. Rate moments 1–5: how exciting it felt. Rate the dull stretches too, not only the highlights – and especially where the meter disagrees with you.
              4. Judge decisions: click an event on the chart (or a man on the pitch), then its choice, timing, direction, power, height – ✓ is praise – and what he could have done instead (to whom: click him). Alt+←/→ steps through them.
              5. Comment with @A9 (click a man), @teamB, @all: what's wrong, what's good. Hover a name and he glows.
              6. Esc saves a .caputrec.gz file – send me its path.

              Aim: 30–50 rated moments and decisions over a few matches.

              What happens with it

              I fit the meter to your ratings (scripts/learn/rate-excitement.mjs --fit) and check whether the model's value of each decision agrees with your judgements. Then step 2: the classic AI copied into a network – neural players you can put in a team, marked 🧠, playing as the classic ones do; and step 3: reinforcement learning towards excitement, one neural player a team first, then more.
